Raid account and communication privacy

Updated October 30, 2026. This describes the development service in this repository. The operator of a hosted service must identify their contact and retention arrangements before public launch.

Accounts and gameplay

The service stores your username, a salted password hash, hashed session credentials with coarse browser/device labels and creation/activity/expiry timestamps, hashed recovery codes and their used status, inventory, progression, party membership, raid checkpoints, results, communication controls and action audit records in its local database. It uses an HttpOnly session cookie to keep you signed in. Passwords and session credentials are not exposed to other players. Device-local settings are separate from server-owned equipment.

Account recovery and sessions

Account security shows up to 20 active sign-ins, using browser/device family labels rather than stored network addresses. Sessions expire after seven days. Password changes require your current password, refresh this browser's session, sign out other sessions, and invalidate all previous recovery codes. Generate a new recovery-code set afterwards. Signing out other sessions also refreshes the current session; individual session revocation requires the current password.

You can generate eight single-use recovery codes after confirming your password. Only code hashes are stored on the server. Plain codes are shown once, never saved in browser storage by the game, and removed from the screen when the panel closes or the page is hidden. Copying them puts them on your device clipboard at your request. Save them privately in your password manager. Replacing the set invalidates every previous code. Recovery uses your username, one unused code and a new password, consumes that code, and signs out every session. Other unused codes from the set remain valid unless replaced or invalidated by a later password change. This service does not provide email recovery.

Session changes, recovery-code replacement and account recovery are audited without passwords, plaintext codes or session credentials. Session revocation stops voice eligibility and requires affected clients to sign in again. Recovery and signing out preserve the existing raid body and equipment risk: they cannot heal, extract, duplicate or refund inventory.

Voice is optional

Voice starts off on each page load. Enabling it asks your browser for microphone permission. While enabled, hold V or Hold to talk to transmit. Releasing the control, changing focus or hiding the page quiets the microphone. Disabling voice or leaving the raid screen stops microphone capture. Your browser's microphone indicator remains on while voice is enabled, including while you are quiet.

Squad voice connects opted-in squadmates. Nearby voice connects opted-in living players within 24 metres, including rivals; choosing Both allows both channels. Nearby voice can identify a consenting speaker without showing their position. The client closes peer connections when eligibility ends. A participant can still record or retransmit what they hear using their own software.

Audio uses encrypted WebRTC connections between participants. The game server relays short-lived connection descriptions and network candidates; it does not record or store voice audio. Peers can learn network addresses through connection negotiation. If configured, STUN/TURN services also process connection metadata; a TURN relay transports encrypted media. The deployment operator chooses those services. The development server configures none by default, which may prevent connections across some remote networks. Connection signaling expires within 12 seconds and is not written to the database or action audit.

Mute, block and report

Mute disconnects voice with that player. Block also suppresses squad pings between you and prevents either account joining a party containing the other. These controls persist across sign-ins. They do not remove a player from an already-running match. You can undo them in the communication panel.

Reports store the reporter and reported account identifiers, the raid or party context, category, your written details, timestamp and a server-derived context summary for operator review. They do not attach audio. Do not include passwords, addresses or other unnecessary personal information. Repeating the same report category for the same player in the same context returns the existing report. A report does not automatically sanction an account. Only a server operator with database access can inspect reports; players cannot read other players' reports through the API.

Operator access and retention

The server operator can inspect saved account/gameplay records and reports, maintain backups and investigate abuse. This development service has no automated personal-data deletion or retention schedule. Public launch requires an operator contact, published retention/deletion process, moderation ownership and a hosting/privacy review. Do not enter sensitive information into report details.
